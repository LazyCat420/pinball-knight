# Peglin — Turning a Physics Toy into a Turn-Based Damage Engine

**Game:** Peglin (Red Nexus Games — Dylan Gedig, Sienna Blumstengel, Lucas Cardoso; Steam Early Access April 25 2022, 1.0 August 27 2024, v2.0 "Act 4" update March 2026 — PC/iOS/Android/Switch)
**Why it is in the research set:** Peglin is the purest existing proof that *ball-vs-peg physics can be the damage formula of a roguelike* rather than a minigame bolted onto one. Every combat number in the game — base damage, crits, AoE, multiball splits, relic multipliers — is generated by an orb ricocheting through a physically simulated pegboard, then reconciled into a deterministic turn-based fight. The game sold [400,000 copies in its first early-access year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peglin) and won [Best Design at the 2022 Taipei Game Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peglin), and its five-year public balancing history (early access → 1.0 → Cruciball 20 → Act 4) is a documented record of how to pace enemy HP against exponentially compounding player physics. For Pinball Knight — which wants pinball momentum to *be* the combat math in real time — Peglin is the reference for per-bounce damage accumulation, special-tile design (crit/refresh/bomb pegs), physics-modifying relics, and the fairness valves (trajectory preview, pity mechanics, caps) that keep chaotic physics feeling like strategy instead of a slot machine.

Primary sources: the [TouchArcade interview with founder Dylan Gedig](https://toucharcade.com/2023/09/11/pegilin-future-content-dlc-pricing-features-cross-save-steam-early-access-community-console-ports-interview/), the [official itch.io devlog](https://gedig.itch.io/peglin/devlog), the ["From Game Jam to winning Best Design" studio story](https://www.gamespress.com/From-Game-Jam-to-winning-Best-Design-The-story-behind-Peglin), the community-maintained [peglin.wiki.gg](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Peg), the [PeglinAssetReader relic dump](https://archindivide.github.io/PeglinAssetReader/) (relic effect strings extracted from game assets), and [PC Gamer's Act 4 coverage](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/nearly-2-years-after-hitting-1-0-goblin-pachinko-roguelike-peglin-adds-a-fourth-act-and-a-final-boss-its-been-a-long-time-coming/). Wiki numbers are community-transcribed from game data and generally reliable; where a claim is community *interpretation* rather than transcription, it is marked as such.

## 1. Core loop — a Spire map wrapped around a pachinko board

### Origin: making arbitrary physics score "mean something"

Peglin started at a 48-hour game jam whose theme was "Fall," which pushed the team toward pachinko/pinball. Per [Gedig's TouchArcade interview](https://toucharcade.com/2023/09/11/pegilin-future-content-dlc-pricing-features-cross-save-steam-early-access-community-console-ports-interview/), after the first day they *had the core physics toy finished* — and the rest of development was the other problem: they "started looking at games like Puzzle Quest for how to make the arbitrary score from popping pegs mean more to the player." That's the founding insight: the physics toy is cheap; the design work is the **exchange rate between physics outcomes and progression stakes**. Red Nexus has also said the single biggest development challenge was [balancing the procedurally generated levels](https://www.gamespress.com/From-Game-Jam-to-winning-Best-Design-The-story-behind-Peglin) — not the physics, the *balance around* the physics.

### Run structure

- **Acts:** three at launch — Forest, Castle, Mines — each ending in a boss; the [Act 4 update (v2.0.3, March 2026) added a fourth act, "The Core," with the game's true final boss](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/nearly-2-years-after-hitting-1-0-goblin-pachinko-roguelike-peglin-adds-a-fourth-act-and-a-final-boss-its-been-a-long-time-coming/).
- **Map:** a branching Slay-the-Spire-style dungeon crawl — fight nodes, elite/miniboss nodes, treasure, and "?" event scenarios that are ["random scenarios that the goblin character faces... typically involving narrative choices rather than combat"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peglin). Even navigation is themed to the toy: [an orb throw is used to pick paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peglin), and at high difficulty a "navigation error" (bad map throw) [costs 4 HP (Cruciball level 4)](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Cruciball).
- **Economy:** after battles you earn gold and can [buy new orbs (25–45 gold by level), upgrade an orb (25+ gold), heal (25 gold), or raise Max HP](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Enemy_Encounter); elites pay out relic choices, bosses pay boss relics.

### The fight loop — physics quarantined inside a turn

Each fight repeats a strict four-phase cycle ([wiki: Enemy Encounter](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Enemy_Encounter)):

1. **Priming** — the next orb in your deck-queue is loaded; you can target an enemy, aim, or discard the orb.
2. **Flight** — the orb is fired into the pegboard and bounces under full physics, activating pegs; each peg hit accrues damage.
3. **Throwing** — when the orb exits the board, it is thrown at the enemies per its throw type (Projectile / Targeting / All) and the accumulated damage lands.
4. **Enemy phase** — enemies act closest-first: attack and/or advance toward Peglin across a lane grid.

When the orb queue empties, the satchel reshuffles ("reload") — and reloading is a real cost: at [Cruciball 15+, enemies take an extra turn during your reload](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Cruciball). Orbs are the *deck*: you add orbs at shops/events, upgrade them (max level 3 for most orbs, [wiki: Orb](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Orb)), and — critically for deck quality — you can **discard a primed orb** instead of firing it, which is Peglin's "skip a bad card" valve.

**The architectural lesson:** Peglin never lets the physics touch the strategic layer directly. Physics runs only inside phase 2; everything upstream (deck, relics, targeting) and downstream (damage application, enemy AI) is deterministic turn logic. The chaos is sandboxed, then *sampled* into one number per shot.

## 2. The damage math

### Per-peg accumulation

Every orb carries two numbers, conventionally written `damage/crit` — e.g. the starting **Pebball is 2/4 at level 1, 3/6 at level 2, 4/8 at level 3** ([wiki: Pebball](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Pebball)). The core formula, as documented on the [Peg page](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Peg):

```
shot damage = (pegs activated) × (per-peg damage value)
per-peg value = orb.damage normally, orb.crit if a Critical Peg was activated this shot
per-peg value is hard-capped at 999
```

- Damage accrues **per peg hit during flight** and is shown live on a damage counter; community guides emphasize [reading the counter, not the raw orb stats](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1296610/discussions/0/505068966120576681), because relic/orb modifiers apply per peg in real time.
- The **999-per-peg cap** is the game's ultimate overflow clamp — no matter how many multipliers stack, one peg contact can never contribute more than 999.

### Critical pegs — retroactive mode-switch, not a bonus tile

The Critical Peg is Peglin's most instructive mechanic and it is *not* a damage tile:

- By default [two crit pegs are on every board and they relocate every shot](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Critical_Peg).
- Hitting one switches the orb's per-peg value from `damage` to `crit` — **retroactively**: ["a Critical Peg can be hit anywhere in a chain and have its effect apply to all pegs hit, past or present"](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Critical_Peg). A crit peg hit on bounce 40 upgrades bounces 1–39 too.
- The crit peg itself [contributes zero damage](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Critical_Peg) (unless the Strange Brew relic is held). It is purely a state flag.
- The retroactive rule is a deliberate anti-frustration choice: the player never has to hit the crit peg *first* for it to count, so a lucky late crit still rescues the whole shot. Order-independence removes the worst kind of physics RNG (same pegs, different order, wildly different damage).

**Stacking:** in the base game, extra crit pegs beyond the first are largely redundant — the flag is already set. Relics exist specifically to monetize the surplus: [**Critsomallos Fleece**](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Critsomallos_Fleece) makes each additional crit add `(crit − damage)` to the per-peg value (changed in v0.7.34 from multiplicative to additive, i.e. the devs nerfed exponential crit stacking to linear), while the rarer [**Ancient Fleece**](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Ancient_Fleece) preserves the old behavior — "Additional crits increase shot damage by **1.3×**," compounding multiplicatively per extra crit — and [**Critikris**](https://peglin.fandom.com/wiki/Critikris) deals `crit × (number of crits hit)` to all enemies. That patch history is a worked example of a studio discovering that *multiplicative crit stacking on top of per-peg accumulation is too explosive to be a common drop* and demoting it to a rare.

### Refresh pegs — the resource loop inside a single shot

Pegs are consumed as they're hit; the [Refresh Peg](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Refresh_Peg) (two per board, random but *stationary* between shots) restores every popped peg (except bombs/crits) when activated. This makes long shots superlinear: a shot that reaches a refresh peg mid-flight gets a fresh board's worth of damage targets. Refresh economy is its own relic axis (see §3).

### Bomb pegs — flat direct damage on a fuse

[Bombs](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Bomb) sit on the board alongside pegs and need **two activations** to detonate (first hit lights the fuse). A standard bomb deals a flat **50 damage to all enemies on screen**; "rigged" red bombs deal **200 to all enemies plus 3 self-damage** (5 at Cruciball 9+). Two physics-relevant details: the detonation *launches the orb at high velocity* (an explosion is also a booster), and [bombs do not count as pegs](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Bomb) for damage tallies or peg-count triggers — they are a parallel damage channel that ignores the multiplier stack, which is exactly why they stay balanced when orb multipliers go exponential.

### Orb leveling and multiball

- Orb upgrades (to level 3) mostly raise the damage pair (2/4 → 3/6 → 4/8) or buff the orb's unique effect ([wiki: Orb](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Orb)). Some orbs invert the pair: [**Critiball** is 0 damage / 4 crit ("every attack is a crit")](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Critiball) and *creates* a persistent crit peg every 8 (→6 at level 3) pegs hit — an orb that farms the special-tile economy itself.
- [**Multiball**](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Multiball) splits an orb in two on the next peg hit, and it is **multiplicative per level**: each stack is another layer of splitting, so Matryorbshka at level 2 splits into orbs that split again (2ⁿ balls). The [Matryoshka Shell relic adds +1 multiball to *every* shot](https://archindivide.github.io/PeglinAssetReader/) and stacks with the orb for up to 4 layers. Peglin's containment valves: split orbs [don't duplicate on-hit status effects](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Multiball), the Matryorbshka [gains no damage from leveling — only more balls](https://peglin.fandom.com/wiki/Matryoshka_Shell), relic-created splits are smaller than the source orb, and **slime pegs suppress the split entirely** (a boss can turn multiball off with board furniture).
- **Damage preview:** the running counter during flight is the "preview" — the player watches damage build per bounce rather than being promised a number up front. Combined with discard-instead-of-fire at prime time, the player always has *pre-shot* agency (skip this orb) and *in-shot* legibility (watch the tally), even though the bounce path itself is chaotic.

## 3. Relic design — persistent modifiers that edit either the physics or the math

Relics are permanent for the run and stack freely. Reading the [full extracted relic list](https://archindivide.github.io/PeglinAssetReader/), almost every relic falls into one of four buckets, and the split is instructive:

### a) Relics that edit the *physics*

- **House of Slime** — "Walls and bottom bouncers are extra bouncy." (Restitution buff = more bounces = more pegs = more damage. Physics change *is* a damage change.)
- **Viridian Trinket** — "Gravity is reversed, but ceiling bounce is massively increased and orbs pass through pegs while travelling downwards."
- **Pumpkin π** — "Each shot, a random slot becomes a portal that can teleport orbs to the top of the pegboard up to 3×." (A pity/extension mechanic dressed as a relic.)
- **Curse of the Peglin King** — alternating shots fire at 2× / 0.5× launch force.
- **Dodgy Shortcut** — +2 crit pegs *but* hitting one slams the orb downward (buys math, sells physics).
- **Unicorn Horn?** — "Your aimer is longer and displays an additional bounce" (buys *information*, see §4).

### b) Relics that edit the *special-tile economy*

Board composition is a first-class stat line: +1/+2 crit pegs (Special Button, Lucky Penny), +1/+2 refresh pegs (Fresh Bandana, PegBag), crit pegs that spawn more crit pegs on hit (Counterfeit Crits — a self-amplifying loop), +3 bombs / 2× bombs / bombs-from-peg-hits every 30 pegs (Bomb Baton, Bombulet, Powder Collector), and deliberately lopsided trades like **Leaf The Rest For Later** — "+10 refresh pegs but each refresh only refreshes 8% of pegs (minimum 5)."

### c) Relics that edit the *math*

Flat orb stat boosts (+2/+0 etc.), bomb damage (+10 / +20 / red-bombs-at-2×), crit stacking rules (both Fleeces, §2), and clean multipliers that always carry a rider: **Endless DevouRing** ("2× damage but every peg activated gets downgraded −3 damage" — the board itself decays), **Wand of Skulltimate Wrath** ("2× damage but 3 self-damage per 30 pegs activated" — damage scaling taxed in HP), **Monster Training** (2× below 25% HP), **Focused Blast** (bombs −2 self-damage and 4× damage, single-target only).

### d) Relics that edit the *turn structure*

Extra orb copies per shot at 0.5× damage and 4-direction aiming (Constricting Chains), multiball every shot at −2/−3 stats (Gopher Gold), thrown bombs re-arming on refresh (Recommodbulator).

### How the game tolerates the compounding

Patterns that go exponential are well known — multiball layers × per-peg-value buffs × refresh loops × crit multipliers is the canonical blow-up, and Ancient-Fleece-style multiplicative crits on a 100+ peg refresh chain is precisely the combo the [v0.7.34 Critsomallos nerf](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Critsomallos_Fleece) took out of the common pool. Peglin's containment stack, assembled from the sources above:

1. **A hard per-peg cap (999)** at the innermost loop — the one place a clamp catches every combo at once.
2. **Riders on every multiplier** — 2× relics cost HP, degrade the board, or narrow targeting; pure multipliers essentially don't exist.
3. **Isolation of channels** — bombs bypass the peg-multiplier stack; multiball doesn't copy status effects; crit pegs deal no damage themselves.
4. **Rarity as a throttle** — the multiplicative-crit relic is rare; the additive one is common.
5. **Boss design that assumes the blow-up** — see §5: the game ships DPS-check bosses (Demon Wall) that are *only* beatable because exponential decks exist, converting "broken combo" into "intended progression."

## 4. Making random physics fair

Peglin's board is genuinely chaotic — as [Adrian Hon's design writeup](https://adrianhon.substack.com/p/peglin) puts it, "it's impossible to predict where the orb will go beyond the first few bounces." The game makes that chaos *feel* fair with a stack of small mechanisms:

1. **Trajectory preview to the first bounce.** The aimer shows the orb's path up to (roughly) its first contact — enough to guarantee the *intent* of a shot (hit that crit peg, drop into that channel), never the outcome. Extending the preview is treated as a real power grant: the [Unicorn Horn? relic](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Unicorn_Horn) explicitly lengthens the aimer and "displays an additional bounce," and the community regards even one extra predicted bounce as [near-overpowered](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FdS3658FoRI). Information about physics is a currency, priced like damage. The aiming system itself got sustained engineering attention across patches ([aimer improvements in v0.5.45](https://gedig.itch.io/peglin/devlog), [prediction-refactor for performance in v1.0.4](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/1.0.4)).
2. **Retroactive crits (§2).** The single biggest fairness rule: bounce *order* doesn't matter for the crit flag, only bounce *membership*. Chaos in sequencing is neutralized; only the set of pegs touched matters.
3. **Designed randomness in layouts.** Boards are hand-shaped compositions (channels, buckets, walls) with randomized *contents* — crit/refresh pegs "override" standard pegs at random positions ([wiki: Peg](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Peg)), crit pegs reshuffle every shot while refresh pegs stay put. So every shot is a fresh tactical puzzle ("route to the crit peg") on a stable, learnable geometry. Balancing these procedural boards was, per the devs, [the hardest problem in the game](https://www.gamespress.com/From-Game-Jam-to-winning-Best-Design-The-story-behind-Peglin).
4. **Floor guarantees.** Every board carries a minimum economy — 2 crit pegs, 2 refresh pegs, bombs spawning alongside pegs — so no shot is ever mathematically hopeless. Difficulty tuning at Cruciball works by *shrinking these guarantees* (−1 crit peg at level 3, −1 refresh at level 5), which confirms they are the designed generosity floor.
5. **Anti-stall, with scars.** Orbs that get physically stuck are despawned, ending the shot — and the community record shows how sensitive this valve is: players hate losing a hot shot to the stall-detector ([\"Ball Shouldn't Delete When Stuck\"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1296610/discussions/0/3276941718963222110/), [respawn-at-top requests](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1296610/discussions/0/3392911947292655808)), and Red Nexus kept patching edge cases as late as [v1.1.18 ("fixed cases where an orb can get stuck bouncing against the wall forever")](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/1.1.18). Lesson: a stall-breaker is mandatory, and *confiscation* (despawn) is its most resented form — redirection (Pumpkin π's portals, bomb blasts flinging the orb) reads far better.
6. **Board manipulation as boss counterplay.** The Slime Boss coats the board in [slime pegs — cannot be hit for damage, kill momentum and bounciness so orbs "fall straight down," and suppress multiball](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Slime_Peg); [slimed pegs](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Slimed_Peg) are the milder variant (normal pegs, slightly deadened bounce). Enemies attacking the *physics field* rather than the player's HP is Peglin's most direct "combat happens on the board" idea — the fairness contract is preserved because the deadening is visible on the board before you shoot.
7. **Player-side outs.** Discard-at-prime (skip the wrong orb), targeting choice before flight, and 4 relic slots of board-composition control mean a bad bounce is a lost turn, never a lost run — variance is amortized over a ~40-shot fight, not concentrated in one roll.

## 5. Enemy and encounter math

Damage exponentiates; HP has to chase it. The documented curve (all values [peglin.wiki.gg](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Green_Slime), standard difficulty):

| Tier | Example | HP | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Act 1 fodder | [Green Slimedrop](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Green_Slime) | 60 (70 Cruciball) | ranged chip damage 1–2 |
| Act 1 boss | [Slime Boss](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Slime_Boss) | 900 (1,080 Cruciball) | 8 melee dmg; fills board with slime pegs; launch point moves between phases |
| Act 2 boss | [Demon Wall](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Demon_Wall) | 7,000 (8,750 at Cruciball 20) | advances 1 space/turn; deals **2,000 unblockable** on contact — a pure DPS race |

- **~15× HP from fodder to act-1 boss, ~8× from act-1 boss to act-2 boss.** By act 2 the game openly assumes the player's per-shot damage has gone superlinear; the Demon Wall is a *timer boss* — its 2,000-damage contact hit isn't meant to be survived, it's a deadline. This is the cleanest documented example of pacing against exponential player scaling: don't cap the player's exponent, **schedule mandatory checks that only the exponent can pass**.
- **Positional pressure:** enemies advance lane-by-lane toward Peglin and act closest-first ([Enemy Encounter](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Enemy_Encounter)); melee contact damage (Slime Boss's 8/turn while it "absorbs" you) makes tempo — kill speed, not just total damage — the real stat.
- **Difficulty as knob-turning, not new content:** the 20 [Cruciball](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Cruciball) levels are a public tuning ledger — worse starting deck (forced Pebball/Terriball/Horriball at 1/11/16), −1 crit peg (3), −1 refresh peg (5), enemy HP up (6), post-battle heal cut to 15% (7), gold payouts cut ~20% (8), boss HP up (10), bombs −10 damage (12), boss heal 50% (13), −15% max HP (14), **enemies act during reload (15)**, +extra spawns (19), bespoke boss buffs (20). Note what they tune: *the generosity floors and the player's economy*, almost never the core damage formula. Community reception shows the limits — threads calling high Cruciball ["not hard, just impossible"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1296610/discussions/0/3276942370877722886/) cluster around the healing nerfs (too many damage sources, no recovery), i.e. attrition knobs sour faster than HP knobs.
- **Elites and events:** elites/minibosses pay relics (the scaling currency) rather than gold, so risk nodes feed the exponent; at Cruciball 2+ even "?" events can spawn elites — the safe path gets taxed.

## 6. Lessons for Pinball Knight

Peglin's mechanics translate unusually directly because Pinball Knight already has the substrate: momentum physics, per-tile surface materials, procedural floors, card-socketing, and a run-scoped skill tree.

1. **Per-bounce accumulation is a proven core damage formula — keep it order-independent.** Peglin's `pegs_hit × per_peg_value` (with a per-contact cap) maps cleanly onto a momentum ARPG: each wall/enemy/prop bounce increments a combo tally, and the tally converts to damage on the next enemy contact (or continuously, with the tally as a multiplier). Steal the two rules that make it *feel* fair: (a) **retroactive modifiers** — a crit trigger hit anywhere in the combo upgrades the whole chain, so bounce order never punishes; (b) **a hard per-contact cap** (Peglin's 999) at the innermost loop so every future stacking bug is pre-clamped. For a real-time game, add the decay Peglin doesn't need: the combo tally should bleed out over ~1–2s without a bounce, making momentum-maintenance the skill.

2. **Special tiles are the special pegs — and Pinball Knight's surface system is already the delivery mechanism.** Peglin gets an entire tactical layer from exactly three special tile types with orthogonal jobs: **crit** (state flag, moves every shot, zero damage itself), **refresh** (restores the consumable board, stationary), **bomb** (flat AoE on a 2-hit fuse, *bypasses* the multiplier stack, launches the ball on detonation). Translation: scatter per-floor special tiles into the maze generator — a crit tile that flags the knight's current combo chain, a "refresh" shrine that re-arms consumed one-shot tiles (boosters, breakables), and fuse-bombs whose damage is flat and multiplier-immune. Two Peglin rules to copy verbatim: special tiles guarantee a *minimum board economy* (never zero on a floor — this is the generosity floor, and difficulty can later shrink it, Cruciball-style), and the volatile one (crit) relocates while the structural one (refresh) stays learnable. Bomb-knockback doubling as a booster is free synergy with the existing launch-chute/booster vocabulary.

3. **Trajectory preview is the fairness valve — and extra preview is loot.** Peglin ships exactly one predicted bounce by default and sells the second one as a relic players consider near-broken. For Pinball Knight: a short aim-line (current heading + first wall reflection, honoring per-tile restitution) during aimed launches/dashes guarantees intent without solving the maze; longer prediction, slow-motion aiming, or post-bounce steering become *rare items with real power budget*. Price information like damage. Corollary from Peglin's stuck-ball saga: ship a stall-breaker from day one, and make it a redirect (portal, kicker, auto-nudge) rather than a confiscation — Peglin's despawn-on-stall is its single most player-resented mechanic.

4. **"Relics that edit physics" is a whole card/item design space — always with riders.** Peglin's best relics change restitution (extra-bouncy walls), gravity (reversed + pass-through), launch force (alternating 2×/0.5×), or board composition (+crit tiles, tiles that spawn tiles) — and *every* strong multiplier carries a rider (HP tax, board decay, aim restriction). Pinball Knight's monster-drop cards socketed into gear are the same slot: cards that raise bounce restitution on stone but not ice, make the third consecutive wall-bounce explode (Kinetic Meteorite pattern: "every third peg hit explodes until a refresh is touched"), convert kill-streaks into spawned crit tiles (Counterfeit Crits pattern), or trade control for output (2× bounce damage, but dash directions locked to 4 — Constricting Chains pattern). Because Pinball Knight has per-tile surface materials, it can do what Peglin can't: physics relics **scoped to a material** ("ice is bouncy," "metal tiles chain lightning per bounce"), which multiplies the design space against the existing surface vocabulary.

5. **Budget exponential stacking deliberately: isolate channels, clamp the inner loop, tax multipliers, and schedule DPS checks.** Peglin's containment stack is a checklist — (1) hard cap at the innermost accumulator; (2) no rider-free multipliers; (3) keep one damage channel (bombs) *outside* the multiplier stack so floors always have a fallback; (4) gate multiplicative stacking behind rarity, ship additive stacking as common (the Critsomallos nerf is the case study: they *demoted* exponential crit-stacking to linear after live data); (5) then embrace the blow-up on the encounter side — Demon-Wall-style timer bosses (7,000 HP, advancing, contact = death) that are only beatable *because* stacked builds exist. For Pinball Knight's boss floors: an advancing/enraging boss that demands the combo engine be online converts "the build went exponential" from a balance failure into the victory condition. And when adding post-game difficulty, follow Cruciball's pattern — shrink generosity floors (fewer special tiles, weaker refreshes, costlier mistakes) rather than inflating the damage formula — but heed the community's verdict there: attrition knobs (healing nerfs) sour players much faster than HP knobs.

6. **Sandbox the chaos.** Peglin's deepest structural trick: physics runs in a bounded phase and is sampled into the strategic layer as a single reconciled number. Pinball Knight is real-time so it can't phase-gate, but the equivalent discipline holds at 60Hz: the *simulation* owns positions and bounces; the *combat math* consumes discrete, order-independent bounce events (tally, flags, material tags) — never raw velocities — so damage stays deterministic, replayable, and network-safe (co-op) no matter what the physics does between events.
